An agent that can publish is an agent that can embarrass you. In Shio the division of
labour is explicit: the agent builds, and **the curator approves, corrects and tweaks**.
Every agent write lands as a **draft** by default; publishing is a separate, gated act;
a destructive operation needs a confirm token naming exactly what it will remove; and
a delete is a trash you can undo, not a hole in the site. Who changed what is
attributed, and a review queue is there for the changes you want to see before they go
live.

The same rule covers what your **visitors** send you: a form submission arrives as a draft post of
a type you already modelled, so it is visible to a curator and invisible to readers until somebody
publishes it. There is no separate submissions inbox to learn, and nothing new decides what
"approved" means, the review queue you already have is the moderation step.

The console is where that curation happens, browse the content tree, edit a post
through the form its post type declares, move and copy with both ends authorized,
compare a draft against what is published and restore an older version, schedule a
publish for later, and manage the media library. For the pages a reader actually
sees, the **Universal Editor** puts the editing surface on the rendered page itself:
click the text, fix the wording, save it back. And a preview link shows the draft
exactly as it will look, before anyone else can see it.

Three pages carry most of that. **[The content console](/docs/content-console/)** is the
screen you are in every day: the browser, the post form, the Media Library, copy and
move, what "select everything" actually selects, and the trash.
**[Preview, history and scheduling](/docs/content-lifecycle/)** is the half of the safety
model that only helps once you know it exists, how to share a draft without publishing
it, what changed since the last publish, and how to put a bad edit back.
**[Content in several languages](/docs/content-i18n/)** is worth reading *before* the second
language, because a translation is a sibling post with its own URL and that decides your
URL structure.
